Multan is the City of Saints, famous for its shrines, blue tile work, sohan halwa and, in summer, the best mangoes in Pakistan. The M-3 has brought it within about four hours of Lahore.

## The drive

The driver joins the **M-3 Lahore to Abdul Hakeem Motorway** just west of Lahore, a short run from Thokar Niaz Baig. It runs south-west across central Punjab to **Abdul Hakeem**, where it meets the **M-4**, which continues past Khanewal to Multan. It is an easy drive, flat and straight, with service areas along the way.

## What to see in Multan

- **Shrine of Shah Rukn-e-Alam:** the great tomb that dominates Multan's skyline.
- **Shrine of Bahauddin Zakariya:** next to the old fort area, visited by pilgrims all year.
- **Ghanta Ghar and the old city:** clock tower, bazaars, blue pottery and sohan halwa shops.
- **Mango season:** June to August, when orchards and markets are full.

## Who books this trip

- **Families** heading to weddings and visiting relatives in south Punjab.
- **Pilgrims** visiting the shrines, especially during urs celebrations.
- **Business visitors** to Multan's industries and offices, often with a night's stay.

## Choosing a car

For up to four people, a Toyota Corolla or Honda Civic with a driver is comfortable for the four hour run. An SUV like the Kia Sportage gives more space for families with luggage. For wedding parties and groups, a [Toyota Hiace or coaster](/van-coaster-rental-lahore/) keeps everyone together, and our [wedding car rental](/wedding-car-rental-lahore/) can cover the couple's car too.
