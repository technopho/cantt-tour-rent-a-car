Lahore to Islamabad is the most travelled intercity drive in Pakistan, and for good reason: the M-2 makes it fast, safe and predictable. Families visit relatives in Islamabad and Rawalpindi, professionals head to meetings in the Blue Area, and travellers catch flights from Islamabad International Airport.

## The drive

From Lahore, the driver joins the M-2 at Thokar Niaz Baig or the Ravi toll plaza, depending on where you are picked up. From there it is motorway almost all the way: flat Punjab farmland past Sheikhupura and Pindi Bhattian, the Jhelum river near Bhera, and then the climb through the **Salt Range** near Kallar Kahar, the most scenic part of the trip.

After Chakri, the M-2 ends near Islamabad. The airport is just off the motorway, and the city centre is about 20 to 30 minutes further, depending on your sector.

## Where to stop

- **Bhera service area** is roughly halfway, with fuel, food and clean washrooms. Most drivers stop here.
- **Kallar Kahar** has a view of the Salt Range and is a good place to stretch your legs. The lake and the peacocks of Kallar Kahar are a short detour off the motorway.
- **Chakri service area** is the last stop before Islamabad, useful if you are arriving for a meeting and want to freshen up.

## Day trip, overnight or one way?

- **Same day return:** possible, but it means about 9 hours on the road. Leave early and keep meetings in the middle of the day.
- **Overnight:** the most relaxed option. The car is yours for the whole stay, and the driver's night allowance covers his stay and meals.
- **One way drop:** ideal for airport drops and for people moving to Islamabad. The return journey is reflected in the price.

## Choosing a car

A Toyota Corolla or Honda Civic is comfortable for up to four adults with luggage. For more space, especially with children or elderly parents, a Kia Sportage or Toyota Fortuner makes the Salt Range climb effortless.
